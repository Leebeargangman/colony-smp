=====================================================================
                 COLONYSMP 2  -  THE NEW PLAYER'S GUIDE
        Found a colony, feed your people, grow, trade and defend
=====================================================================

This guide takes you from nothing to a working town. Part 1 gets you
started in ten minutes; dip into the later parts when you need them.
There is a cheat sheet at the end.

Every command starts with /colony (or /col). /colony help shows them
all in game, and /colony help <topic> explains one area.

=====================================================================
PART 1 - YOUR FIRST TEN MINUTES
=====================================================================

STEP 1 - Found the colony
  Type:   /colony create <name>
  You get a Town Hall Core (a lodestone), a Building Marker (a light
  blue banner) and a Blueprint Book.

STEP 2 - Place the Town Hall Core
  Walk to where your town should stand and place the core. Pick flat,
  open ground with room around it: everything else is built nearby.

  * The 3 x 3 chunks around the core become your land.
  * A storage chest appears next to the core. It is the colony's
    storage, shared by everyone.
  * Four settlers arrive: a Farmer, a Builder, a Forester and one
    without a job yet. They bring bread, seeds and saplings to last
    a few days.
  * You get a 72-hour peace shield: nobody can declare war on you.

  A boss bar shows your first steps. Finishing them sends tools,
  seeds and food to storage.

STEP 3 - A Roof Overhead (house your settlers)
  Build a small closed room: walls, a roof, a wooden door and 2 beds.
  At least 3 x 3 inside is enough. Light it with a torch.

  Place the Building Marker anywhere inside it. It works out that the
  room is a house and registers it:

  "Detected a House! Registered House #2 with 2 beds." Two settlers
  now have a home. Break the marker again and keep it for the next
  building: the house stays registered.

  (The Blueprint Book does the same: stand inside, book on House,
  SHIFT + RIGHT-CLICK the floor, or /colony building register house.)

STEP 4 - Break Ground (a farm)
  Till a patch of farmland, 3 x 3 or bigger, with water nearby.
  Place the Building Marker on the farmland: it registers a Farm.
  Or look at it and type:
      /colony building register farm

  Your Farmer takes the job straight away and starts sowing with the
  seeds from storage.

STEP 5 - Stock the Stores
  Have 32 food in storage. The settlers' bread may already be enough.
  To add more, open the storage chest and click food in your own
  inventory to put it in.

That's it: you have a colony. Right-click the Town Hall Core to open
the Town Hall menu, where you can reach almost everything else.


=====================================================================
PART 2 - THE TOWN HALL AND YOUR LAND
=====================================================================

The Town Hall menu (right-click the core, or /colony menu):
  Citizens, Jobs, Buildings, Storage, Economy, Policies, Military,
  Diplomacy, Construction, History, Statistics, Members, Recruit a
  settler, Upgrade the Town Hall, and your first steps.

/colony info is the short version: population, beds, food in days,
treasury, daily costs, stability, mood, storage, and the colony's
biggest problems.

LAND
  Stand in a chunk next to your land and type /colony claim. Each
  claim costs a little more than the last. How many chunks you can
  hold depends on the settlement level. /colony map shows the land
  around you. Colonies keep a gap of 2 chunks between them.

  On your land, outsiders can't build or open your doors and chests.
  Creepers and fire don't damage it, and nobody can trample your
  farmland.

THE SETTLEMENT LEVEL
  The Town Hall's level is the colony's level (1 to 5). No building
  can be a higher level than the Town Hall. Upgrade it from the menu
  (the beacon) when you have enough people and materials: level 2
  needs 5 citizens, 500 crowns, 64 stone bricks, 64 planks, 8 iron
  ingots and 16 glass.


=====================================================================
PART 3 - BUILDINGS: BUILD, REGISTER, ORDER
=====================================================================

A building only works once it is registered and passes inspection.

BUILD IT YOURSELF, THEN REGISTER
  1. Build it: a closed room with what the type needs (a door, the
     right workstation, beds...). Fields, pens and yards are fenced or
     made of farmland instead.
  2. Place the Building Marker inside it (on the farmland for a farm,
     inside the fence for a pen or yard, on top for a watchtower). It
     works out what the building is from what's in it - a bed makes
     a House, a lectern a School, an anvil and furnace a Blacksmith -
     and registers it. If the room could be more than one thing (a
     furnace suits a Kitchen and a Bakery), a menu asks which. If it
     isn't anything yet, it says what's missing for the closest type.
       Right-click the marker      inspect it again
       Shift + right-click it      choose another type
       Break it                    the building stays registered;
                                   the marker drops for next time
     Lost it? /colony marker gives another (or craft one: any
     banner, paper and a gold nugget makes 2).
  3. Or do it by hand with the Blueprint Book: turn it to the type
     (right-click to turn pages, or /colony book <type>) and
     Shift + right-click inside (or /colony building register <type>).

  Examples of what level 1 needs:
    House         bed, wooden door, 9 floor tiles
    Farm          9 farmland
    Forestry Hut  door, chest or barrel
    Bakery        door, furnace / smoker / campfire, 9 floor
    Trading Post  door, cartography table, chest or barrel
    School        door, lectern
    Clinic        door, bed, brewing stand
    Warehouse     door, 2 chests or barrels
    Prison        iron door, bed
  /colony help buildings, the book's pages and /colony building <id>
  show the rest.

LET THE BUILDERS BUILD IT
  House, Farm, Warehouse, School, Prison and Watchtower have standard
  plans. Turn the book to one, look at the ground where it should
  stand, and SHIFT + LEFT-CLICK, or type:
      /colony construction order <type>
  It's built facing the way you look. The book shows an outline:
  green fits, red doesn't. Your builders fetch every block from
  storage and tell you what's missing. /colony construction shows the
  orders, and you can pause, resume, cancel or move one to the front.

COPY YOUR OWN DESIGNS
  The Copy Wand (/colony wand) copies a building you built into a
  plan. Your builders can then build copies of it anywhere.

CHECKS AND UPGRADES
  Buildings are inspected every day. One that fails stops working
  and says why. Fix it, or it is unregistered after 2 days.
  /colony validate checks now. /colony upgrade <id> raises a building
  a level once it has what that level needs.


=====================================================================
PART 4 - YOUR CITIZENS AND THEIR JOBS
=====================================================================

Right-click a citizen (or /colony citizen <name>) to see who they are:
job, home, needs, mood and why, skills, education, trait, family,
and their history. /colony citizens lists everyone.

GIVING JOBS
  /colony assign <citizen> <job> [building id]
  e.g.  /colony assign Rhea Pike farmer
  Most jobs need a workplace with a free place: farmers need a farm,
  bakers a bakery. /colony jobs lists the open places.

  An Administrator at the Town Hall (it needs someone with some
  schooling) finds work for the unemployed every morning.

WHAT THE JOBS DO
  Food:    Farmer, Animal Keeper, Fisher, Miller, Baker, Butcher,
           Cook, Restaurant Worker, Food Distributor
  Wood and stone:  Forester, Miner, Carpenter, Mason
  Crafts:  Blacksmith, Craftsworker, Engineer
  Town:    Builder, Warehouse Worker, Merchant, Shopkeeper,
           Administrator, Treasurer, Innkeeper
  People:  Teacher, Doctor, Warden, Bard
  Learning and craft:  Researcher, Enchanter
  Nature:  Druid, Beekeeper
  Defence: Guard, Soldier
  Students and children go to school; the Unemployed wait for work.

  Citizens get better at the work they do. Some jobs need schooling:
  Administrator 35, Teacher 30, Doctor 40, Enchanter 45,
  Researcher 50.

A CITIZEN'S DAY
  They work during working hours (a policy), have free time in the
  evening and sleep at night. If they get hungry they fetch a meal,
  if they're exhausted they nap, and if they're hurt they go to the
  clinic. When danger comes they shelter, run or fight, depending on
  your emergency policy.


=====================================================================
PART 5 - FOOD
=====================================================================

Everyone eats about two loaves of bread a day. /colony info shows how
many days of food you have, and the daily report warns you when it
runs low.

  Farms    grow wheat, carrots, potatoes or beetroots. Choose with
           /colony building crop <id> <crop|auto>.
  Mill     grinds wheat into flour.   Bakery   bakes bread and more.
  Pens     raise cows, sheep, pigs and chickens. Lead the animals in.
  Fishery  needs open water nearby.   Kitchen  cooks fish and meals.
  Butcher  turns surplus animals into meat.
  Restaurant  better meals: happier people, and income.

Better and more varied food makes people happier. Rationing (a
policy) stretches the food at the cost of mood. Starving citizens get
weak and then die.


=====================================================================
PART 6 - STORAGE AND CARRYING
=====================================================================

Storage is counted in slots like a chest: one stack of one good per
slot. The Town Hall gives 27 slots per level. A Warehouse adds 27 for
every chest or barrel inside it, and a Granary holds food and crops.

Open the chest next to the core to put goods in (click them in your
own inventory) or take them out. /colony resources lists everything.

Workers carry what they need from storage and bring back what they
make. Until you have Warehouse Workers or Food Distributors, everyone
does their own carrying, which takes time away from their work.

Stock targets tell the colony what to keep:
    /colony storage target bread 64 256
Workshops make goods below the minimum first, and merchants sell
anything above the maximum.


=====================================================================
PART 7 - MONEY AND TRADE
=====================================================================

The treasury pays wages (tax is taken off) and building upkeep every
day. Workers spend their wages at the market and on meals, so much of
it comes back. If the treasury runs dry, unpaid workers lose heart and
then strike, and buildings fall into disrepair. /colony economy shows
the books.

  Trading Post + Merchant:  /colony trade  sells surplus and buys
      what you lack. Prices drop when you sell a lot and recover over
      time. Food below your food-reserve policy is never sold.
  Market + Shopkeeper:  citizens spend wages there, and other players
      can buy from your colony with /colony shop <colony>.
  /colony deposit <amount>  puts your own money in (Vault money, or
      emeralds if the server has no economy plugin).
  Auto-sell and auto-buy (policies) let merchants trade by your stock
  targets every day.


=====================================================================
PART 8 - KEEPING PEOPLE HAPPY
=====================================================================

Mood comes from needs: food, rest, health, a home (a good house beats
a crowded one), company, safety and work. Wages, taxes, working
hours, food variety, illness and grief move it too. The citizen menu
says exactly why someone is happy or not.

Stability is the colony's mood over time. When it's low, people
steal, quarrel, protest, strike and riot, and the miserable leave.
Guards and a staffed prison keep crime down.

Health: hurt citizens heal in bed and faster at a clinic with a
doctor. Illness spreads where people are crowded or hungry, and
doctors cure it with medicine. Quarantine (a policy) slows an
outbreak but keeps the ill at home.

Policies (/colony policies or the Town Hall) set wages, tax, working
hours, recruitment, births, military service, the defence posture,
the emergency response, production priority, the food reserve,
rationing, quarantine and trade. Each one says what it costs.


=====================================================================
PART 9 - GROWING THE COLONY
=====================================================================

Every new citizen needs a free bed and a day's food.

  Recruit a settler from the Town Hall menu (/colony settler). It
      costs money, and more as you grow.
  Travellers stop by the Town Hall now and then. Right-click one, or
      /colony traveller recruit, to take them in. An inn makes them
      stay longer. Attacking them ruins your reputation.
  Children: couples with a home, enough food and a good mood have
      children. Children grow up in a few days and go to school if
      there is one.

Schools: a teacher educates children and students. Bookshelves make
lessons better. Education makes people better workers and opens up
skilled jobs.


=====================================================================
PART 10 - MEMBERS AND ROLES
=====================================================================

  /colony invite <player>       they type /colony join <colony>
  /colony promote <player> [role], /colony demote <player> [role]
  /colony members, /colony kick <player>, /colony leave

Roles: Owner, Co-owner, Manager, Resident. Allies (members of allied
colonies) may use your doors. /colony settings shows who may do what,
and /colony settings perm <role> <permission> on|off changes it.


=====================================================================
PART 11 - GUARDS, SOLDIERS AND SQUADS
=====================================================================

GUARDS
  Build a Watchtower (a tower at least 5 blocks tall, or order the
  plan) and assign guards to it. They watch the land, fight monsters
  and intruders, and keep crime down.

SOLDIERS
  /colony recruit <citizen> <class>
  /colony recruit lists the 28 classes and what each needs: militia
  need nothing, swordsmen a Training Ground, archers an Archery Range,
  bows and arrows, and cavalry Stables and a war horse. Gear comes from
  the armoury. Soldiers train daily, cost upkeep and sleep in
  Barracks (4 can serve without them).

  Classes counter each other: spears and pikes stop cavalry, shields
  stop arrows from the front, axes break shields, maces beat armour,
  cavalry wreck archers and flanks.

SQUADS
  /colony squad create <name>
  /colony squad add <squad id> <citizen>
  /colony orders <squad id> <order>        e.g. follow, hold, charge
  Orders: defend, hold, follow, patrol, charge, shield wall, spear
  wall, volley, focus, flank, retreat, protect, hold fire.
  Formations: line, column, wedge, square, loose
  (/colony squad formation <id> <formation>).

  The Command Baton (/colony baton):
    right-click a block          rally the squad there
    Shift + right-click          next order
    left-click an enemy          everyone attacks it
    Shift + left-click           next squad

  A squad with broken morale runs. Standard bearers and commanders
  steady them, and losing badly shakes everyone.

  /colony mobilize stands everyone to arms for 10 minutes, at a cost
  in stability.


=====================================================================
PART 12 - OTHER COLONIES: DIPLOMACY AND WAR
=====================================================================

/colony diplomacy (or the Town Hall) lists the other colonies and how
you stand with them, from allied to at war.

  /colony diplomacy ally <colony>              propose an alliance
  /colony diplomacy treaty <colony> <treaty>   non_aggression,
        trade_agreement, defensive_pact, open_borders
  /colony diplomacy gift <colony> ...          money or goods
  /colony diplomacy trade <colony> ...         a trade offer
  /colony diplomacy offers                     what's waiting for you
  /colony diplomacy accept <colony>            (or click [Accept])

WAR
  /colony declarewar <colony> raid     a short raid: stand at their
        storage to loot it, and defeat their people for points.
        The higher score wins.
  /colony declarewar <colony> siege    raise a War Camp with the War
        Banner (/colony banner) near their land. Win by destroying
        their Town Hall Core or cutting them off from their stores.
        They win by holding out or tearing down your camp.

  War costs money, needs one of their members online, and ends your
  own peace shield. There's a warm-up first so both sides can get
  ready: stock the armoury, because guards and soldiers draw arms when
  it starts. Blocks broken in a war are put back afterwards, and the
  loser gets a peace shield.

  /colony peace <colony> offers a truce. /colony surrender gives up.


=====================================================================
PART 13 - PRISONERS
=====================================================================

In a fight, enemies (and travellers) may go DOWN instead of dying.
Hit a downed body again and they die.

  1. Craft Rope: 3 string in a column (makes 2).
  2. Right-click the downed enemy with Rope to bind them. They follow
     you.
  3. Lead them to the door of one of your Prisons. They're locked in.
     (Or Shift + right-click them to leave them for your wardens.)

Wardens feed prisoners and wear down their resistance each day. When
it breaks, the prisoner joins your colony. Prisoners from another
colony can be ransomed or exchanged instead:
  /colony prison                        list them
  /colony prison ransom <name> [price]
  /colony prison exchange <ours> <theirs>
  /colony prison release <name>


=====================================================================
PART 14 - RESEARCH, REQUESTS, RAIDS AND OTHER HAPPENINGS
=====================================================================

Once the colony feeds itself, there is plenty to do. /colony today
shows what is going on right now.

REQUESTS
  Citizens ask you for things from time to time: "Could someone
  bring me 8 cookies?" /colony requests lists them. Fetch what they
  ask for, hold it, and right-click the citizen. They pay you from
  their own purse, cheer up, and work harder for a couple of days.
  Nothing bad happens if you don't, but they notice.

RESEARCH
  Build a University: a room with a lectern and 8 bookshelves. Put a
  Building Marker in it and choose University. Assign a Researcher
  (education 50 or more), then pick a project with /colony research.
  Projects cost money and goods (mostly paper and books) and take the
  researchers a few days. Each one changes the colony for good:
  faster farmers, bigger stores, tougher citizens, smaller raids.
  Bigger universities unlock the later projects.

THE RARER TRADES
  Tavern (seats, a barrel, a note block or jukebox) and a Bard:
    music in the evening, and everyone is happier.
  Library (6 bookshelves and 2 seats): adults read and learn.
  Druid's Grove (a fenced garden with a gate and 2 mossy stones)
    and a Druid: crops grow faster.
  Enchanter's Tower (an enchanting table and 4 bookshelves) and an
    Enchanter: your people's tools and weapons get enchanted. Keep
    lapis lazuli in storage.
  Apiary (a fenced yard with a gate and 2 beehives) and a
    Beekeeper: honeycomb, and honey if there are glass bottles in
    storage. You bring the bees.

VISITORS AND WINDFALLS
  Every few days something happens: a festival with fireworks, a
  spell of good growing weather, a lucky strike in the mine, stray
  animals, a travelling scholar. A merchant's caravan sometimes
  stops at the Town Hall for a few minutes: right-click the merchant
  to buy animals (they come on a lead: walk them home), bees, and
  things you can't make. Refugees may ask for shelter:
  /colony refugees accept (they need beds and food) or refuse.

RAIDS
  When the colony has 6 citizens and is 5 days old, raiders start to
  take an interest. You are warned in the morning: "Scouts report a
  band of barbarians... They will attack at nightfall."

  Barbarians want your stores. Any who reach the storage chest carry
  goods away. Kill them all and you get everything back, plus a
  bounty. You can pay them off before they come:
  /colony raid tribute.

  The undead want your people. A Necromancer leads them and keeps
  raising more. Kill the Necromancer and the rest fall down.

  Get ready during the day: post guards on watchtowers, recruit and
  arm soldiers, /colony mobilize when it starts, and be there. The
  raid ends at sunrise whatever happens. You can't sleep through
  it. Raids never come while you're offline.

MILESTONES
  /colony milestones lists goals with rewards: 10 citizens, a first
  raid beaten, a first discovery, and more.

LISTEN TO YOUR PEOPLE
  Citizens speak to you as you walk past. Mostly they tell you what
  is wrong: "I'm stuck: waiting for 6 flour", "I haven't been paid
  in 2 days", "I slept on the ground again". It's the quickest way
  to find out what the colony needs. /colony chatter off if you'd
  rather they kept quiet.

CHRISTMAS (1 December to 6 January)
  /colony christmas gives the colony a Christmas Tree Sapling (or
  craft one: spruce sapling + gold nugget + red dye). Plant it on
  grass under open sky in your land and it grows into a tree with
  lanterns and a star. While it stands everyone is merrier, the
  bard plays carols, the children throw snowballs, and Father
  Christmas visits every few days: right-click him for a present,
  then right-click the present to unwrap it.


=====================================================================
PART 15 - WHEN NOBODY IS AROUND
=====================================================================

Your colony doesn't stop when you log off. Citizens near players
walk around as villagers; everyone else is simulated: they eat,
sleep, work, get paid, fall ill, have children. Farms, pens,
fisheries, apiaries and workshops keep producing from real inputs,
and researchers keep researching. Foresters,
miners and builders need someone nearby to work, because they change
the world.

Time follows the world's clock, so nothing happens while the server
is off. Each morning there's a report of the day: /colony events and
/colony stats.


=====================================================================
PART 16 - TROUBLESHOOTING
=====================================================================

"Registered" but nobody works there
  Is there a free worker? /colony jobs. Assign one, or get an
  Administrator.

The building "failed its inspection"
  /colony building <id> says what's missing. Doors and beds must be
  inside the room, and the room must be closed.

Builders aren't building
  /colony construction: they're probably waiting for a material.
  Put it in storage.

"No free bed" when recruiting
  Soldiers need beds too. Build or extend a house.

Nobody eats / food is gone
  Food in a farm's own stock isn't in storage yet: workers carry it
  over. Check /colony resources and /colony production.

Can't sell food
  The food-reserve policy keeps a few days of food back. Change it
  in the policies.

Research isn't moving
  It needs a Researcher at work in a University big enough for the
  project. /colony research shows both.

The marker says School when I wanted a University
  A room with a lectern and bookshelves can be either. Shift +
  right-click the marker and choose.

The beekeeper collects nothing
  Hives fill only when bees live in them and flowers grow nearby.

Citizens are standing around at night
  They have free time in the evening and sleep after dusk. Check the
  working-hours policy.
